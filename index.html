LinkedIn: quick fixes

  1. Your headline names one role, plus your edge"AI Engineer · LLM evals & cloud infra", not five keywords separated by pipes.
  2. The headline isn't a job request"Seeking new grad roles" makes your job hunt the headline instead of what you can do. The green Open to Work frame is fine.
  3. Featured shows something you builtSomething a Hiring Manager can click and use: a live demo, a shipped app, real users. An empty Featured section is wasted space.
  4. Every role has a description, especially the current one60% of the profiles I sampled left their current role blank or one line. Recruiters search that text; 2–4 bullets with numbers, same as your resume.
  1. Your name and titles match your resumeA different surname or a stale current role makes a recruiter wonder if it's the same person.
  2. About opens with what you buildNot "passionate recent graduate". Three lines: what you build, the proof, what you want next.
  3. Your top 3 skills are AI Engineering skillsThey're pinned near the top, so they act as a second headline. "Figma, Product Strategy" or "Leadership, Entrepreneurship" pulls you away from the role.
  4. No fancy Unicode fonts"Bold" and "italic" look-alike characters pasted into About get stripped in exports and likely in search. They also break screen readers. Use plain text.

Resume: quick fixes

  1. The title names the role you wantAnd so does the file name. "Resume_SWE_New_Grad_V6.pdf" gets read too.
  2. Your strongest AI work is in the top thirdNot bullet 3 of job 2.
  3. Every skill appears in a bulletIf "MLOps" lives only in the skills list, it reads as keyword stuffing.
  1. Dates and titles survive a reference checkNo unexplained overlaps. Keep official titles and add a descriptor instead of renaming.
  2. Strong metrics state how they were validated0.90 AUC on 86 samples needs "held-out subjects, 5-fold CV".
  3. It reads cleanly as plain textPaste your PDF into a text editor. If it's scrambled, applicant tracking systems scramble it too.
